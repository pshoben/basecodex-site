The short version

  • Podyglot helps you learn to understand spoken French.
  • Your lessons, answers and scores stay on your iPhone and in your own iCloud account. We cannot see them.
  • We run a small subscription service. It keeps a record of your subscription, your Podyglot name, your invites, circles and classes, the level numbers you reach, and, only while you are on a family board, a short summary of your progress for the people on it. It knows you by a code, not by your name or email address.
  • We never receive your Apple ID, your email address or your payment details. Apple handles all payments.
  • There are no adverts in Podyglot. We do not track you across other apps or websites. We do not use analytics companies. We do not sell your data.
  • If the app crashes, iOS may give the app a technical crash report, and the app sends it to us. See section 4.9.
  • Other learners see your Podyglot name (for example "Flying Green Pig 284"), never your real name. If you join a class, your teacher sees a little more. If you join a family board, the people on it see your level and a summary of your progress. See section 5.
  • You can ask us for a copy of your data, and ask us to correct or delete it. Sections 11 and 12 explain how, and what deleting means for each part.

1. Who we are

Podyglot is made by BaseCodex, in the United Kingdom.

We are the "controller" of the personal data described in this policy. That means we decide why and how it is used, and we are responsible for it under UK data protection law: the UK GDPR and the Data Protection Act 2018.

  • Email: stan@basecodex.com

We have not appointed a Data Protection Officer, because the law does not require one for an organisation like ours.

2. What this policy covers

This policy covers:

  • the Podyglot app for iPhone, from the App Store, and
  • the Podyglot subscription service that the app talks to, at entitlements.basecodex.com.

Apple runs the App Store, payments, iCloud and your iPhone. Apple's own privacy policy covers what Apple does with your information: https://www.apple.com/legal/privacy/

3. A few words we use

  • Podyglot account code. A long code made from the identifier that iCloud gives Podyglot for your iCloud account. It contains no name and no email address. iCloud gives each developer's apps a different identifier, so the code means nothing outside Podyglot.
  • Podyglot name. The name you choose when you first open the app: three words and a number, for example "Flying Green Pig 284". It is how other learners see you. The app also calls it your avatar.
  • Household. People who share one Podyglot subscription through Apple Family Sharing. Someone on their own is a household of one.
  • Circle. A group of households who share the lower "circle price".
  • Class. A group a teacher sets up. Pupils ask to join it.
  • Family board. A group the person who pays for Podyglot sets up, so the people they pay for (or anyone they choose) can see each other's progress. You are on it only if you ask with its code and they let you in. It has nothing to do with Apple's Family Sharing.

4. What we collect, and why

4.1 Your learning progress

What. Your lessons, quiz and exam answers and results, the words and phrases you have met, how long you studied each day, your plan days, levels and streaks, your exam course choice, and your settings. Each record also carries a random code for the device it came from, so that progress from two devices adds up correctly.

Where. On your iPhone. If you are signed in to iCloud, it is also stored in your own private iCloud storage, so it follows you to your other devices.

Who can see it. Only you. It is kept in your iCloud account, not on our computers, and we cannot read it. The only part our service learns is the level number you reach (section 4.7), and, only while you are on a family board with someone else, a short summary of your progress (section 4.8).

Why. To run your course and keep it the same on all your devices.

If you are not signed in to iCloud, your progress stays on that iPhone only.

4.2 Your subscription

Apple sells Podyglot subscriptions and takes your payment. Apple keeps your payment details. We never see them.

When you start a subscription or free trial, renew, change plan, turn off auto-renew, get a refund, or redeem an offer, Apple sends our service a signed record of it. When you are signed in to iCloud, your app also sends our service the signed records your iPhone holds. These records include:

  • which Podyglot plan you have (for example the standard price or the circle price) and the dates it runs,
  • Apple's transaction numbers,
  • whether you bought it yourself or receive it through Family Sharing,
  • whether it will renew,
  • the App Store country, the price and the currency,
  • any free trial, offer or refund, and
  • your Podyglot account code, when you subscribed in the app while signed in to iCloud.

Why. To unlock Podyglot on all your devices, to work out the circle price and free months fairly, and to keep an accurate record of who has which free offers.

4.3 Your Podyglot account code, and the device check

When you use the app while signed in to iCloud, it sends our service your Podyglot account code. That is how our service knows that requests come from the same learner.

The app also uses Apple's App Attest to prove that each request comes from a genuine copy of Podyglot on a genuine Apple device. Your iPhone makes a security key that never leaves it. Our service keeps the public half of that key, Apple's statement about it, the date, and which learner the key acts for. Each request also says which build of the app sent it.

If you sign in to a different iCloud account, Podyglot treats you as a different learner and makes a new key.

Why. To stop fake requests, to protect free offers from abuse, and to make sure nobody can act as another learner.

4.4 Your Podyglot name

When you first open the app, you choose your Podyglot name. Our service adds a number and keeps it. You can hold up to four names over time. You can change the one you use at most once every 30 days.

A Podyglot name is never given to anyone else, even after you stop using Podyglot. To make sure of that, we keep every name we have given out. See section 9.

If you are not signed in to iCloud, your name is kept against your iPhone's security key instead of your account code.

The words come from lists we chose to be suitable for children.

You can see your name in Settings, under "My Podyglot name".

4.5 Invites, circles, households, gift tokens and free periods

If you use these features, our service records:

  • invites you send: when, into which circle, the invite code, and whether and when your friend opened it in the app and claimed it,
  • invites you claim, and the one-time Apple offer code you were given,
  • the circles your household made or joined, and when you joined or left,
  • which Podyglot accounts share a subscription through Apple Family Sharing, which our service works out from the signed records your family's apps send,
  • your household's free month and any other free periods,
  • gift tokens: who earned them, who gave them to whom, and certificates made and redeemed,
  • any role we gave you, for example as a tester or a teacher, and
  • if we have to stop an account from sending invites or gifts because of abuse, that we did so and why.

Why. To run these features as the app describes them, and to stop the same free offer being claimed twice.

Invite messages. When you share an invite, you choose the app that sends it, for example Messages or WhatsApp. The message contains your Podyglot name and the invite code. That app's own privacy policy applies to the message.

4.6 Classes: pupils and teachers

A teacher gets a teacher code from us and sets up a class. Pupils ask to join with a six-letter class code.

If you are a pupil and ask to join a class, you give us:

  • your class, for example "S1B",
  • your first initial, and
  • the first three letters of your surname.

When your teacher lets you in, we add a four-digit number, for example "S1B M-SMI-4821". We also keep when you asked, whether your teacher let you in, turned you away or took you out, whether you left, and whether you pressed "Let my teacher know".

We never ask for your full name. Your teacher can type your name next to your mark in their own app. That note stays on the teacher's iPhone. Podyglot never sends it to us or to anyone else. (Like other app data, it is part of the teacher's own iPhone backup.)

If you are a teacher, we record your teacher code, your class, its join code and seats, whether your free term renews, and the actions you take in the class.

Why. So teachers can give pupils free access and see who is in their class.

4.7 Levels you complete

Your course is divided into levels. When you complete a level, the app tells our service the language and the level number. Our service records the time. Nothing else about your learning is sent.

Why. Completing a level can earn your household a gift token. If you are in a class, your teacher sees your level.

4.8 Family boards

The person who pays for Podyglot can open a family board and send its code to whoever they like. You join only if you enter the code, ask, and they let you in. The app tells you exactly what is shared before anything is sent.

What. For the board: who opened it, its code, who asked to join, who was let in, turned down, taken off or left, and when. For each member, while they share a board with someone else, a summary their app works out: the level they are on, their plan day in each mode, how many of the last seven days they studied, today's progress rings, the number of words they have met in each mode, and the day it was worked out. Only numbers and a few fixed words. Never your answers, the words or phrases themselves, or your subscription.

Who can see it. Everyone on the same board, including the person who opened it, sees each member's Podyglot name, the level they last completed and when, and their summary. The person who opened it also sees who is asking to join, by Podyglot name. Nobody else sees anything. A name you type next to a member stays on your iPhone and is never sent.

How long. Only the latest summary is kept, never a history. It is deleted as soon as you no longer share a board with anyone: when you leave, are taken off, or the board is closed. The record of who joined and left is kept with our other records (section 9).

Why. So the person who pays, and the family they choose, can see how everyone is getting on. Being on a board gives you nothing else: it does not share a subscription (only Apple's Family Sharing does that) and it does not count towards a circle.

4.9 Crash and diagnostic reports

If the app crashes, freezes, uses too much of your iPhone's processor, or writes too much to its storage, iOS may later give the app a diagnostic report. We understand that iOS does this only if you have turned on Share With App Developers (Settings, Privacy & Security, Analytics & Improvements).

The app sends each report to a database we control inside Apple's iCloud. A report contains:

  • your iPhone model, iOS version and the app's build number,
  • the date, and a short summary such as "1 crash", and
  • Apple's technical report: where in the app's code the problem happened, and technical details such as memory use and your region format setting.

A report contains no learning data, no name and no email address. But iCloud notes which iCloud user saved each report, using the same identifier your Podyglot account code is made from. So a report can be linked to your Podyglot account code.

A report can only be saved when you are signed in to iCloud.

Why. To find and fix bugs.

Apple may also give us crash reports and app usage statistics through App Store Connect, if you chose to share them with app developers. Apple gives us these in a form we cannot link to you.

4.10 Daily listening content

The app checks for new daily listening content when it opens, when you come back to it, and every five minutes while it is open. It downloads the content from a public website hosted by GitHub. These requests carry no account information. Like any request on the internet, they show GitHub your IP address and basic technical details, such as the app's name and the iOS version. GitHub's own privacy statement applies to that.

Why. To give you each day's new listening content without an app update.

4.11 Reminders and notifications

Daily reminders are off until you turn them on. Your iPhone schedules them itself. Nothing about them is sent to us.

The app also receives silent notifications through Apple's iCloud, so it can fetch the progress you made on your other devices. The app does not send us your notification token.

4.12 If you contact us

If you email us, we keep your email address, your message and our reply, so we can help you and keep a record of what we agreed.

4.13 Test versions

If you test a pre-release version through Apple's TestFlight, Apple shares with us the name and email address it invited you with, and any feedback, screenshots or crash reports you send. Test versions talk to a separate test copy of our service. They also keep a technical log on your iPhone, which leaves it only if you choose to send it to us.

4.14 What we do not collect

  • No adverts, no advertising identifier, and no tracking across other companies' apps or websites.
  • No third-party analytics or advertising code in the app.
  • No location from your iPhone, and no contacts, photos, camera or microphone. The app does not listen to you. (Apple's purchase record does include your App Store country, section 4.2.)
  • We do not receive your Apple ID, email address, phone number, payment details or real name. The one exception is the class details in section 4.6.
  • Our subscription service does not store your IP address. It uses it only briefly, in memory, to limit how many requests one connection can make. Cloudflare, which passes traffic to our service, also sees it (section 7).

5. What other people can see

  • People in your circles see your Podyglot name. They also see whether the circle has a subscribed friend in it, which keeps the circle price on. In a small circle, members may be able to work out from this whether another household is subscribed.
  • People you invite see your Podyglot name, in the invite message and in the app when they open the code.
  • Someone who invited you sees your Podyglot name once you accept, and that you opened their invite in the app.
  • People you give gift tokens to see your Podyglot name. You see the names of the people in your circles you can give to.
  • Your household (Family Sharing) shares its circles, its free month and its received gift tokens.
  • Your teacher, if you join a class, sees:
    • your class, your initial, the first three letters of your surname and your number,
    • whether you are asking to join, in the class, or out of it,
    • whether you have claimed your class pass, and then whether you are on a free period, subscribed, or lapsed,
    • your level, and when you last reached one, and
    • whether you pressed "Let my teacher know".
  • People on your family board, if you join one, see your Podyglot name, the level you last completed and when, and the summary in section 4.8. The person who opened it also sees that you asked to join.
  • Nobody else sees your lessons, your scores or your answers.

We (BaseCodex) can see everything our subscription service holds, through an admin console. We use it to run the service, to help you, and to deal with abuse. We cannot see your lessons, scores or answers in iCloud.

7. Who we share it with

We do not sell personal data. We do not share it with advertisers or data brokers.

These companies help us run Podyglot:

  • Apple: App Store payments, iCloud storage (including crash reports), the App Attest device check, and notifications. Apple is also responsible in its own right for its own services.
  • Fly.io: runs the computers for our subscription service, in London and Amsterdam.
  • Neon: runs the database for our subscription service, on Amazon Web Services in London.
  • Cloudflare: protects our subscription service and passes traffic to it. To do this it decrypts and re-encrypts each request.
  • GitHub: hosts the daily listening content and our website.
  • Zoho Mail: holds emails you send us.

We may share information if the law requires it, or to protect the rights or safety of our learners, other people or BaseCodex.

If Podyglot or BaseCodex is ever sold or reorganised, this information may pass to the new owner, who must keep to this policy.

8. Where your information is stored

  • On your iPhone.
  • In Apple's iCloud data centres, which may be outside the UK.
  • Our subscription service: computers run by Fly.io in London (UK) and Amsterdam (Netherlands), and a database run by Neon on Amazon Web Services in London (UK).
  • Cloudflare handles traffic in many countries, usually close to you.
  • GitHub's servers may be outside the UK.

Some of these companies are based in the United States and may access data from there. When data leaves the UK, we rely on the UK's adequacy regulations for the Netherlands and the rest of the EEA, and for US companies the UK Extension to the EU-US Data Privacy Framework or the International Data Transfer Addendum, as each supplier offers.

10. Children and young people

Many Podyglot learners are at school, studying for GCSE French. We built Podyglot with that in mind.

  • You do not need to give your real name. Podyglot names come from word lists chosen to be suitable for children.
  • Joining a class needs only your class, your first initial and the first three letters of your surname. The app tells you what your teacher will see before anything is sent.
  • There are no adverts, no tracking, no location, no chat and no public profile.
  • Reminders are off until you turn them on.
  • A family board shows its members your level and a summary of your progress. You are on one only if you ask and are let in, the app says exactly what is shared first, it is always listed in Settings (Family board), and you can leave whenever you like.
  • Podyglot is for learners aged 13 and over. Younger children may use it only with a parent's or teacher's help.
  • Apple accounts for children under 13 are set up by a parent through Family Sharing, and purchases can need a parent's approval through Ask to Buy.
  • A parent or carer can contact us to use their child's rights (section 11).
  • Teachers: if you use Podyglot with a class, please check your school's own data protection policy.
  • We aim to follow the ICO's Age Appropriate Design Code (the Children's Code).

11. Your rights

Under UK data protection law you have the right to:

  • ask for a copy of your personal data,
  • ask us to correct it,
  • ask us to delete it, in some cases,
  • ask us to limit how we use it, or object to how we use it, in some cases,
  • ask for it in a form you can take elsewhere, in some cases, and
  • complain to the Information Commissioner's Office (ICO).

How to ask. Email stan@basecodex.com. Tell us your Podyglot name (Settings, "My Podyglot name"). Other learners can see Podyglot names, so we may ask for something more to check that the data is yours.

We reply within one month. It is free.

Complaints. We would like the chance to put things right first. You can also complain to the ICO: ico.org.uk, or 0303 123 1113.

12. Deleting your data

There is no button in the app that deletes your data. This is what you can do today.

On your iPhone. Delete the app. This removes your learning progress and settings from that iPhone. iOS may keep a few small technical items in the iPhone's keychain, such as a random device code and a reference to the security key. They contain no name and are used only if you install Podyglot again.

In iCloud. First delete Podyglot from every device that has it. If a device still has your progress, it will upload its copy again. Then, in the Settings app, tap your name, then iCloud, then Manage Account Storage, then Podyglot, then Delete Data from iCloud.

Your subscription. Cancel it in the Settings app: tap your name, then Subscriptions. Cancelling stops payments. It does not delete any records.

Family boards. Leaving every family board you are on (Settings, Family board) deletes your progress summary from our service at once.

Our subscription service. Deleting the app or your iCloud data does not touch what our subscription service holds. For that, email stan@basecodex.com with your Podyglot name. Our records are append-only (section 9), so deleting them is a manual job for us. We will remove, or permanently disconnect from you, the entries that relate to you. We may keep what we must, for example to honour a subscription or free period that is still running, to deal with a refund, or to make sure the same free offer is not claimed twice. Your Podyglot name will be retired and never given to anyone else. We will tell you what we kept and why.

Crash reports. Email us and we will delete the reports linked to your Podyglot account code.

Information Apple holds, such as your purchases and your Apple Account: see privacy.apple.com.

13. How we protect your information

  • Every connection is encrypted.
  • Our subscription service's computers have no public internet address. They can only be reached through Cloudflare.
  • Every answer our service sends is signed, and the app checks the signature.
  • App Attest proves each request comes from a genuine app on a genuine device.
  • The service can add and read its records, but it cannot change or delete them. The exceptions are marking an offer code as given out, and replacing or deleting family board progress summaries (section 4.8), which are kept apart from its records for exactly that reason.
  • Offer codes are encrypted in our database.
  • Only we can use the admin console, from devices we have approved.

No system is perfectly secure. If a breach puts your information at risk, we will tell you and the ICO when the law requires it.

14. Decisions made automatically

Our service applies the app's rules automatically. For example, it works out whether your circle qualifies for the circle price, whether your household has had its free month, and it blocks a device for a while if it keeps sending invalid requests. None of these has a legal effect on you. If you think one is wrong, email us and a person will look at it.
